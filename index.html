Helping more women find Friends That Invest when they start investing

Friends That Invest teaches investing to women through a weekly newsletter, a podcast and a Masterclass. This case study looks at why women searching their first money question don't find Sim, and how FTI could change that using content it has already made.

13/18real beginner questions were some form of "where do I start?"
1/26podcast episodes (Apr to Oct 2026) were about getting started
0/5Google and ChatGPT searches showed FTI for beginner questions

in short

  • The gap: FTI writes beginner explainers every week, but they live in inboxes and paid products, so search engines and AI tools can't see them. A competitor with a smaller audience shows up instead.
  • What I did: audited 26 newsletter issues, collected 18 real beginner questions, ran a search test and reviewed a competitor.
  • The idea: turn what FTI already has into short "question pages" that answer first, link to Sim's own material, and end with a newsletter sign-up.

why this case study

I'm the reader FTI was built for.

I grew up being told I didn't need to learn about money because my husband would handle it. Sim's content was one of the first places that made finance feel like a room I was allowed into. Today I'm the more financially literate person in my marriage.

In August, Friends That Invest featured my story as the reader story in Stock Market Tea. This case study is my way of giving something back: using my background in user research and content to help more women like me find Sim.

research · October 2026

02

What I did

Method 1

Newsletter audit

Read all 26 Stock Market Tea issues sent between 20 April and 12 October 2026 and logged each one: explainer, podcast episode, reader story, Masterclass push and sponsors.

Method 2

Question mining

Collected 18 real beginner questions, word for word, from Google's "People also ask" and Reddit beginner communities, then grouped them by theme.

Method 3

Search test

On 14 October 2026, searched three of those questions in Google (private window) and ChatGPT, and noted whether FTI appeared.

Method 4

Competitor review

Looked at Female Invest, the women's investing platform that appeared in my searches, to see how they get found.

findings

03

Four problems, and the evidence for each

Problem 1

Her best beginner content disappears every week

Every issue of Stock Market Tea includes a plain-language explainer. It's the part that never goes out of date, and it's exactly what beginners search for. But once the email is sent, it's gone.

  • About 217 issues sent; 0 readable on FTI's website (no public archive on the site, Kit or Substack).
  • 26 of 26 explainers link readers to Investopedia, not to a page FTI owns.
  • 16 of the 26 explainers are beginner level, for example Passive Investing, Market Order and Dividend Yield.
Problem 2

Women searching beginner questions find other brands

These are the exact questions FTI exists to answer, and Sim covers them in her podcast and Masterclass. None of FTI's content showed up.

Problem 3

What beginners ask isn't what the podcast covers

Problem 4

Between launches, there's no path to the Masterclass

The Masterclass is FTI's one paid offer, and it opens three times a year. In between, nothing on the website brings in new readers who could become the next students.

  • 18 of 26 issues don't mention the Masterclass at all; the other 8 cluster around launches in late April, mid July and early October.
  • FTI's free tools (an investment calculator and an "invest or pay off debt" flowchart) exist, but are only reachable from one Resources page.

What FTI already does well

The raw material is already there, so the fix doesn't need new content from scratch:

A plain-language explainer every week, for over four years.

A real reader story every week. Five of 26 were women unlearning what family or culture taught them about money, mine included.

Five years of free podcast episodes, many on beginner topics.

Free tools that are a natural next step for a beginner.

The opportunity: FTI doesn't need more content. It needs the content it already has to live somewhere search engines and AI tools can find it.

the question to solve

How might we help a woman searching her first money question find Sim's answer, and give her a reason to stay for the next one?

solution

05

Question pages built from what FTI already has

One short page per beginner question, published on FTI's existing Shopify site. Each page is assembled from Sim's own explainers, episodes, tools and reader stories, so the advice stays hers.

The question as the headline

In the words beginners actually type.

The answer first

A short answer and the first steps on one phone screen.

Numbered steps, each with its source

Every step names the episode, interview or issue it comes from.

FTI's own glossary

Terms open FTI's explainers instead of Investopedia.

The podcast inside the steps

The right episode at the moment it helps.

A next step

The free calculator, then a Stock Market Tea sign-up.

prototype

06

One question page, built in FTI's style

I built one page for the question "How can I start investing with less than $100?", in FTI's own fonts and colours.

Everything on it comes from FTI's public content or Sim's published interviews, with quotes word for word. Where her advice isn't public (for example, how she compares platform fees), the page says so and flags it to confirm with her.

A small pilot FTI can measure

No redesign needed. FTI's Shopify site already includes a blog, so pages can go live without new tools.

honest caveats

08

Limits of this research

  • 18 questions is a small sample. The pattern is clear, but a larger set would make it stronger.
  • "Covered by Sim" means covered in the 26 issues and episodes I audited, not across five years of podcast episodes.
  • Search results change by day, location and account. The search test is a snapshot from 14 October 2026.
  • I didn't have access to Sim's book or Masterclass, so the prototype only uses her public content.
  • Theme tags are my own judgement. The full data is in a spreadsheet I'm happy to share.
